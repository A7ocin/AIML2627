6.4.1 · The kernel trick

What are Lagrange multipliers?

Give each training point one nonnegative parameter, called a Lagrange multiplier. Its value weights that point’s margin constraint.

𝒂=(a1,…,an)ai≥0for everyi

From multipliers to a separator

In the dual problem, we solve for 𝒂 first. The optimal multipliers let us recover the weights 𝐰 and the bias w0 that solve the original SVM problem, called the primal.

Why subtract each constraint term?

The margin rule is yisi−1≥0. A point that falls short has a negative value inside the parentheses. Subtracting ai(yisi−1) then raises the Lagrangian when ai>0.

ψ maps a point into the lifted space. κ computes the corresponding dot product from the original inputs. For a large feature map, a kernel that is cheap to evaluate can save work.
6.4.1 · The kernel trick

The quadratic kernel saves work

For a point with m original features, the quadratic map includes every squared feature and every scaled product of two distinct features.

Quadratic feature map

xi↦(xi12, 2xi1xi2, …, 2xi1xim,xi22, 2xi2xi3, …,…, 2xi,m−1xim, xim2)

Build m(m+1)2 coordinates per point, then take the dot product of the expanded vectors.

Equivalent quadratic kernel

κ(xi,xi′)=(xi⊤xi′)2

Take the dot product of the original inputs and square it. That gives the same result in 2m operations per pair, using the original m coordinates.

Optional derivation · Not required for study

The two √2 factors give 2 for every distinct feature pair. Grouping those terms gives the square of the original dot product.

ψ(xi)⊤ψ(xi′)=∑j=1mxij2xi′j2+∑j=1m∑k>j(2xijxik)(2xi′jxi′k)=∑j=1mxij2xi′j2+2∑j=1m∑k>jxijxi′jxikxi′k=(xi⊤xi′)2=κ(xi,xi′)
Gaussian kernel
κ(xi,xi′)=exp(−γ‖xi−xi′‖2)
It computes a dot product in an infinite-dimensional feature space from the original points’ squared distance. The parameter γ > 0 controls how quickly the value falls with distance.

Four questions about nonlinear SVMs

  1. The inner class reaches radius 1.2. The ring starts at radius 1.5. Choose a height threshold z = t for z = x₁² + x₂² and justify it.
  2. Two training points have labels −1 and +1. Their dual multipliers are 0.4 and 0.6, respectively. If the first stays fixed, what must the second be to satisfy the bias condition?
  3. The feature map gives ψ(x) = (2, 1) and ψ(z) = (−1, 3). What must a kernel matching this map return for κ(x, z)?
  4. Where does the kernel enter the dual? Which parts of the optimization stay the same?
Check your understanding

Work through the four answers

  1. The possible threshold range is 1.44 < t < 2.25. For example, t = 2 puts the highest inner point below the plane and the lowest ring point above it.
  2. The bias condition is −0.4 + a₂ = 0, so a₂ = 0.4.
  3. Compute the inner product of the mapped coordinates. κ(x, z) = 2 × (−1) + 1 × 3 = 1.
  4. Replace each mapped inner product with the kernel value. The multipliers, labels and constraints stay the same.
